Challenge 03 · Change scale using evidence

How big is this, really?

Keep the large circle and the three smaller circles inside it. Imagine a scene smaller than a fingernail, human-sized, or larger than a building; develop one using details that establish its scale.

Your challenge

  1. Keep all four original circles visible and in the same relative positions.
  2. Add lines, colour, texture or collage; do not erase or cover a starter outline.
  3. Rotate or uniformly resize the whole starter only. No independent movement of circles.
  4. Imagine something smaller than a fingernail, something human-sized and something larger than a building. Develop one.

Beginner route: list three ideas, then add just three clues to one. Drawing skill is optional; words, simple sketches and collage count.

Try before you peek?

Pause here if you want to discover your own interpretation first. When you are ready for inspiration, open this challenge’s section in the gallery. There is no single correct answer.

Optional progressive hints
  1. Name your first assumption.
  2. Change scale using evidence. What evidence would make that reading plausible?
  3. Add one detail that changes the meaning of the whole image.

Reflect

What detail told you how big the scene was? Remove it mentally: does the scale change?

Why this challenge?

After context in Challenge 01 and negative space in Challenge 02, test how surrounding evidence changes perceived size.

Share when ready

Keep your sketch and a sentence about what changed. To submit for review, open Share yours, select this challenge and sign in with Google. You can share your own work on Instagram and tag @trymanymaybe. Tagging does not automatically add your work to the gallery.
